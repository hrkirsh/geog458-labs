# Port Fourchon Vessel Activity (August 26-30,2021)

## Study area and dates 
The data was gathered from AccessAIS in the Port Fourchon, Louisianna region between the dates 8/26/2021-8/30/2021. This area and date range was sepcifically chosen to observe the impact of Hurricane Ida on vessel activity. Port Fourchon is a regional hub for vessels thanks to its offshore oil rigs, and was directly hit by Hurricane Ida on August 29, 2021. While the selected date range is rather long, the file size itself was managed by the decreased vessel activity in response to the storm. 

## Interpretation: 
There are various patterns among time, routes, and vessel types. For example, there appear to be around 5-6 different passenger routes, compared to a single cargo route that seems to be coming and going from the same oil rig. The "other vessels" category shows how the Offshore Support Vessels (OSV), etc., travel between oil rigs and back and forth from the port itself. On the 27th, two days before Ida makes landfall, the magnitude of trips increases specifically along Southwestern routes. By evening (17:00 CST), the vast majority of vessels travel straight away from the port without returning. By 2:00am CST, very few vessels leave from or return to the port. For the rest of the time period, there is no vessel activity in the region. Additionally, it appears as though the number of passenger vessels briefly increase shortly before the hurricane makes landfall. 

Source: https://www.npr.org/2021/08/31/1032738395/hurricane-ida-does-more-damage-at-port-fourchon-than-previous-storms
